# GENAI-DECLARATION.md — Generative AI use declaration

The assessment brief requires **exactly one** of the two statements below to appear in
your final submission, depending on whether you used Generative AI (GenAI) tools while
completing this assessment. The brief's wording for both statements is reproduced here
verbatim.

**Instructions**

1. Read both statements.
2. Decide, honestly, which one is true for you and your teammate.
3. Delete the other one. **Do not submit both.**
4. Replace the `<...>` placeholders in the statement you keep with your real answer —
   an unedited template is not an acknowledgement.
5. Paste the surviving statement into the place your submission requires (your report
   or the assessment cover/documentation file — see
   [SUBMISSION-CHECKLIST.md](SUBMISSION-CHECKLIST.md)).
6. Keep this file in `docs/` as the record of what you declared, with the same wording.

> **This assessment is GenAI Level 2** (Purpose-Specific GenAI Use Permitted). That
> means the tools are allowed for a defined list of purposes, and *not* for others.
> Getting the purpose clause wrong is itself a problem: it is a declaration.

---

## Option A — If you DID use GenAI tools

The brief gives this statement exactly as follows:

> I acknowledge that I have used GenAI tools to complete this assessment. I used
> \<GenAI tool(s)\> to \<specific purpose(s) of using GenAI\> within the parameters
> outlined in the Assessment Brief and by the Unit Assessor.

Copy it and fill in the two placeholders. **Keep the sentence intact** — do not reword
it, shorten it or merge it with anything else.

### What the brief permits at Level 2

You may use GenAI to:

* brainstorming ideas or ideating;
* checking grammar;
* paraphrasing, editing tone or clarity;
* formatting a document;
* formatting references or citations;
* designing layout templates.

You may **not** use GenAI to:

* create your report;
* generate text and/or code and paste it into your report or code.

So a purpose clause describing permitted use looks like brainstorm, grammar, tone,
formatting, references or layout templates. A purpose clause that says you generated
your report or your code describes a **prohibited** use, and declaring it accurately
will not make it acceptable — it will simply be an admission. If that is what happened,
talk to the Unit Assessor before submitting.

### Example A1 — the full statement, filled in

A concrete, permissible example. Adapt it so it describes what **you** actually did;
replace the tool name with the one you really used.

> I acknowledge that I have used GenAI tools to complete this assessment. I used
> **ChatGPT (GPT-4)** to **brainstorm ideas for the charity events website pages and to
> check the grammar and clarity of my written documentation** within the parameters
> outlined in the Assessment Brief and by the Unit Assessor.

### Example A2 — a second permissible phrasing

> I acknowledge that I have used GenAI tools to complete this assessment. I used
> **Microsoft Copilot** to **brainstorm the layout of the admin dashboard and to
> improve the tone and clarity of the comments in my own code** within the parameters
> outlined in the Assessment Brief and by the Unit Assessor.

### Example A3 — more than one tool or purpose

List them plainly; the statement does not require formal prose.

> I acknowledge that I have used GenAI tools to complete this assessment. I used
> **ChatGPT and Grammarly** to **brainstorm feature ideas, check the grammar of this
> README, and format the tables in our documentation** within the parameters outlined
> in the Assessment Brief and by the Unit Assessor.

### Writing your own purpose clause

Answer three questions, then write the answer as a short phrase:

1. **Which tool(s)?** Use the real product name — "ChatGPT (GPT-4)", "GitHub Copilot",
   "Claude", "Grammarly", "Microsoft Copilot".
2. **For what?** Use language from the permitted list above: *brainstorm / ideate*,
   *check grammar*, *paraphrase or improve clarity*, *format the document*, *format
   references*, *design a layout template*.
3. **On what part of the work?** Be specific — "the search page wireframe", "the
   wording of the README", "the table layout in our documentation".

Good:

* "…to brainstorm ideas for the event registration page and check the grammar of my
  documentation…"
* "…to design the layout template of the admin dashboard before I built it…"
* "…to paraphrase and tighten the wording of the setup instructions I had written…"

Avoid:

* "…to create my report…" — prohibited use.
* "…to generate my code…" — prohibited use.
* "…to help with the assignment…" — too vague to be a real acknowledgement.
* "…to do my research…" — ambiguous, and easy to read as the report being written for
  you.

## Option B — If you DID NOT use GenAI tools

The brief gives this statement exactly as follows:

> I acknowledge that I have not knowingly used GenAI to complete this assessment.

If you did not use any GenAI tool for any part of the assessment, submit this statement
on its own. Note the word **knowingly**: if a tool you used has AI features you did not
think about — an editor's AI autocomplete, for instance — consider whether it produced
any of your submitted text or code, and choose the statement that is actually true.

## Why the declaration matters

The brief states this warning directly:

> IMPORTANT: Misuse of GenAI or failure to acknowledge its use may breach academic
> integrity rules. The Unit Assessor may also ask you to describe or demonstrate which
> GenAI tools you used, how you used them, and how your use complied with the
> assessment guidelines. Be ready to discuss this if asked.

In practice, that means:

* **Be accurate.** An under-stated declaration is a false declaration.
* **Be ready to explain.** You may be asked to demonstrate how you used a tool, or to
  explain any part of your code. Keep your prompts and outputs if you used GenAI, so
  you can show exactly what was assisted and what was your own work.
* **Do not paste generated text or code into your submission.** At Level 2 that is
  explicitly prohibited, and it is the thing markers look for: a mismatch between the
  quality of the code and your ability to explain it in the video or an interview.
* **Declare consistently across the team.** Each student submits their own declaration,
  so check that you and your teammate are not submitting contradictory statements about
  the same shared work. If one of you used a tool on a shared file, both declarations
  should reflect that.

The assessment also carries SCU's **Academic Integrity Declaration**, which every
submission makes automatically:

> I have read and understood SCU's Academic Integrity policies and referencing
> guidelines. I am aware of the consequences of academic misconduct and confirm that
> this submission is my own original work, referenced appropriately, and has not been
> previously submitted. I authorise its reproduction for authentication purposes and
> understand the implications of a false declaration. I have adhered to guidelines
> regarding Generative AI.
