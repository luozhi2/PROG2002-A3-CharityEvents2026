# Peer Evaluation — guidance notes

The official blank form is `Peer Evaluation form.docx` as supplied on Blackboard under the
Assessment 3 folder. A pre-filled working copy is included at
`docs/Peer Evaluation form - COMPLETED.docx`.

> **Read this first.** The completed copy was filled in as a *template*. Every rating and every
> sentence about team dynamics is **sample wording, not a record of what actually happened**.
> You must replace the `[SQUARE BRACKET]` placeholders and rewrite the free-text answers so they
> describe your real team. Submitting someone else's account of your group, or ratings you do
> not actually stand behind, is a breach of the academic integrity declaration you sign when
> you submit.

---

## 1. How the form works

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Who fills it in | **Each** team member completes it **individually and confidentially**. Do not fill in one shared copy. |
| Whose names go on it | Your own name, then **each other group member in a separate column**. For a two-person team that is two columns: you and your partner. |
| Rating scale | STRONGLY AGREE (5) · AGREE (4) · NEUTRAL (3) · DISAGREE (2) · STRONGLY DISAGREE (1) |
| Rate against | The six evaluation criteria printed down the left of the table |
| Totals row | Add up each column. Six criteria at 5 each gives a maximum of 30. |
| Workload allocation | The percentage of the project each member contributed — the two figures should add up to 100% |
| Team dynamics | Three free-text questions at the bottom |
| Where it goes | **Directly to your tutor, not to UA** |
| Email subject | `PROG2002_A3_[Team_ID]_[Member_name]` — for example `PROG2002_A3_GC1_NehemiaSugianto` |
| When | Before the assessment due date |

Note the form's own instructions still say `PROG2002_A2_...` in one sentence; the examples and the
assessment brief both use **A2 → A3**, so use `PROG2002_A3_...` in your subject line and mention
the discrepancy to your tutor if they ask.

## 2. The six criteria, in plain English

1. **Attends group meetings regularly and arrives on time.** Did they turn up, and on time?
2. **Contributes meaningfully to group discussions.** Did they add ideas, not just agree?
3. **Carries out tasks without overly depending on other team members.** Did they get on with their part?
4. **Demonstrates quality work assigned based on the role agreed.** Was their work up to standard and within their role?
5. **Demonstrates a cooperative and supportive attitude.** Were they easy to work with? Did they help others?
6. **Contributes significantly to the success of the assessment.** Overall, did the project succeed partly because of them?

## 3. Rating honestly

A useful rule of thumb, so your numbers are defensible if your tutor asks:

| Score | Means |
| --- | --- |
| 5 — Strongly agree | Consistently exceeded expectations; you would name them as a strength of the team |
| 4 — Agree | Reliable and solid throughout; no concerns |
| 3 — Neutral | Adequate, but inconsistent — occasional missed meetings or late work |
| 2 — Disagree | A recurring problem that the team had to work around |
| 1 — Strongly disagree | They did not meaningfully contribute |

Most healthy teams land in the 4–5 range. Awarding yourself or your partner all 5s is common but
stands out; a mix of 5s and 4s reads as more considered. Be honest about a genuine problem — the
form exists precisely so the tutor can see how the team really worked, and a candid 3 with a clear
explanation is more credible than a false 5.

## 4. Workload allocation

State a percentage **and** what each person actually did. Vague entries ("did some of the work")
are not useful. Example of the level of detail expected:

- **50%** — database schema and seed data, all REST API endpoints, the Angular client website
  (home, search, event details, registration page), API testing, cPanel deployment
- **50%** — admin-side Angular website (event list, create/update/delete forms, dashboard),
  GitHub repository and commit history, demo video recording and editing

Percentages must total 100%. If the split was genuinely uneven — say 65/35 because one person was
unwell for a fortnight — say so and explain it. That is not a failure; hiding it is the problem.

## 5. Team dynamics — sample answers

**These are templates. Rewrite them so they are true for your team.**

### How effectively did your group work?

> Our group worked well overall. We agreed on the scope in the first week, split the work so that
> one of us owned the client website and the other owned the admin website, and shared the API and
> database work. We used a shared GitHub repository and committed our changes regularly, which made
> it easy to see what the other person had done. When we disagreed about the database design we
> talked it through in the tutorial and agreed on a solution that satisfied both of us.

### Were the behaviours of any of your team members particularly valuable or detrimental to the team? Explain.

> Both of us contributed valuable work. [Partner] was particularly valuable in setting up the
> GitHub repository, keeping the commit history clear, and building the admin-side interface, which
> meant the admin website was finished before the deadline. Our regular check-ins helped us find
> integration problems early. Neither of us was detrimental to the team, and when either of us fell
> behind we told the other early so the work could be redistributed.

If there *was* a problem, describe the behaviour and its effect on the project rather than the
person's character. For example: "For the first two weeks we had no agreed task list, which meant
we duplicated work on the event page. Once we created a shared task board in week 3 this stopped."
That is specific, fair, and useful to your tutor.

### What did you learn about working in a group from this assessment that you will carry into your next group experience?

> I learned that agreeing on the interfaces between components first — the database schema and the
> exact JSON the API returns — saves a lot of rework, because both of us could then build our pages
> independently without waiting for the other. I also learned to commit to GitHub in small, clearly
> described steps and to communicate early when a task is taking longer than expected. I will carry
> all of these habits into my next group project.
